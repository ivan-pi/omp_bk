Fraction of FP32 peak (19.7 TFLOP/s): below 2% at every order; the kernel is
memory-bound and the % of copy is the meaningful figure.

What was measured on the way (p = 2..7 unless noted):
- G factor-major (layout 0), per-plane reads, rows written into flat planes:
  78-95% of copy; odd nq ~78%, ~2000 movs per element at nq = 7 (unaligned
  overlapping row destinations).
- Rows written into padded planes: movs 1959 -> 640 at nq = 7, speed unchanged.
- A build without arithmetic (same loads and stores) ran at the same speed:
  memory access pattern is the limit, compute is hidden.  With cache-resident
  loads the kernel runs at 61-106 GDoF/s.
- G in layout 1 (one contiguous run per plane) and out packed per element with
  aligned block writes: odd nq 78-80% -> 88-89%.  Kept.
- Not kept (no gain at p = 3..7): EPT > 1 (consecutive or strided), two G
  buffers (prefetch two planes ahead), 2-4 planes of G per load, a 7:1
  read/write yardstick (BK5 itself beats it, 433 vs 412 GB/s).
- Rotating the plane order per element (e mod 8) lifted a memory-only probe
  at nq = 6 from 90% to 99% of copy, at nq = 8 only 91%; not implemented in
  the kernel (needs a rotated basis copy and per-plane stores).

p = 8 (nq = 9): the register kernel spills ~11 KB at nq = 9 (4.1 GDoF/s, 30%).
Replaced by the SYCL scheme in CM: a work-group of nine threads per element,
thread i owning plane i, U and R shared through SLM, two barriers.  One
element per group (SLM 8.4 KB) 11.1 host / 12.2 device GDoF/s; two and three
elements per group 10.5 and 10.0 (fewer groups per Xe-core).
